The initiative

NativData Tech started from a simple observation. In several areas that shape everyday life in Nigeria, the data is missing, spread across separate sources or hard to combine. That makes the people affected hard to see.

I take one gap at a time, analyse what the data does show, and document it so others can check the work and build on it. The current projects cover language and AI, and health.

Language and AI, financial inclusion

Dataset in development

Project 1. Nigerian Pidgin for fintech customer support

Conversational AI used in banking and fintech often fails on Nigerian Pidgin, even though many customers speak it.

I am building a structured Pidgin conversation dataset for fintech support scenarios. Each conversation is labelled for intent, emotion and context, reviewed by native speakers, and paired with audio aligned to the approved text. Complexity is added in stages.

The dataset is in development (access upon request). It has not been released publicly.

The public companion is a geospatial analysis of Pidgin distribution against financial inclusion across Nigeria.

Project 2. Nigeria NCD geospatial analysis

Maps type 2 diabetes burden and cancer registry coverage using published epidemiological sources.

The maps show a South to North pattern in diabetes, and how limited the geographic coverage of cancer surveillance is in the sources I used.

Health, data integration

Published

Project 3. NCD indicators from fragmented health data

Analysis of diabetes and hypertension related indicators from the NDHS 2023-24, for women and men across the six geopolitical zones.

The focus is on what becomes visible when the sources are combined, and what fragmentation still blocks. The women's and men's files name the same questions differently, which is a small example of the problem.

Project 4. Predictors of blood sugar measurement

A logistic regression on NDHS 2023-24 data. The outcome is whether a person has ever had their blood sugar measured.

Wealth and education were the strongest factors after adjustment. Urban residence was not significant once those were accounted for.

The aim is to show who is least likely to be screened, so limited screening effort can go where it is needed. It is not a diabetes diagnosis model.

How the work is done

Start from real data
National surveys and published epidemiology, with the source named every time.
Say what the data cannot show
Survey health indicators are mostly self-reported. They show who was diagnosed or screened, not the full disease burden.
Publish the evidence
Code goes on GitHub. Reports go on Zenodo with a DOI, so anyone can cite and check them.
Release in stages
Anything still in development is labelled that way and is not released until it is ready.
